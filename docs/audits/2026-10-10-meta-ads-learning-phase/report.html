The short answer

It is partly the budget and partly how the campaign is managed. At ₹1,850 a day, no manager could get out of learning. But the ad set was also reset about 9 times in 25 days, and the new setup is doing worse than the old one on every efficiency number. Learning alone will not fix ROAS. Five changes will.

1Budget is too small for purchase learningLearning needs ~50 purchases a week. We get 6. That would cost ₹7K to ₹12.5K a day.
2The ad set never gets a clean weekTargeting, budget and creative changes every 2 to 3 days restart learning each time.
3"Broad" targeting is not broad8 cities, automatic audience off. The old all-India setup got 2.4x the clicks per view.

So, is it the new manager?

Partly. Here is the split.

✓Not their fault

  • Budget vs cost: any manager would sit in learning at ₹1,850/day.
  • ROAS was already weak before they joined (0.67).
  • Festive season: ad costs are high (₹229 per 1,000 views).
  • Short time: 3.5 weeks and ₹38K is a small sample.

!Their part

  • ~9 resets in 25 days: no week ever ran clean.
  • Narrowed a "broad" campaign: automatic audience off.
  • Creatives swapped in the live ad set instead of tested separately; best ad paused for 2 days.
  • New ₹300/day ad set is iOS-only and optimises for add to cart, so it can never learn.

Question 3

When can we see real return on ad spend?

Not on the current setup. Exiting learning usually trims cost per purchase by 20 to 40%. We need roughly 5x. If the changes below start this week:

By ~17 Oct
First clean 7 days. Cost per purchase should head back toward ₹1,000.
Late Oct to early Nov (Diwali)
2 to 3 stable weeks. Prospecting ROAS ~0.65+, retargeting clearly above 1.
Mid to late Nov
Decision point. Still above ₹1,200 per purchase after 4 clean weeks means the ad or offer is the problem, not learning.
