Two numbers that matter more than the front of the pack

Words like "protein rich", "power snack" or "healthy" on the front of a pack do not tell you much. Turn the pack over and look for two things.

1. Protein per 100g

Every packaged food in India has a nutrition table. Find the protein line in the "per 100g" column. This is the fairest way to compare two snacks, because some packs list a small "per serving" amount that makes the number look different.

  • Under 10g per 100g: a light snack, not a protein snack.
  • 10g to 20g per 100g: a fair amount of protein.
  • Over 20g per 100g: a lot of protein for a snack.

These bands are a simple rule of thumb, not an official standard. In India, the Food Safety and Standards (Advertising and Claims) Regulations, 2018 decide when a pack may say "source of protein" (at least 10% of the Recommended Dietary Allowance per 100g of a solid food) or "high protein" (at least 20% per 100g).

2. Protein per ₹100

This tells you how much protein your money buys. The formula is simple:

Protein per ₹100 = (protein per 100g × pack weight in g ÷ 100) ÷ price × 100

Here is an example with made-up numbers, just to show the maths:

Example snack Pack weight Protein per 100g Price Protein in pack Protein per ₹100
Snack A 200g 20g ₹150 40g 26.7g
Snack B 100g 40g ₹250 40g 16g

Both packs give you 40g of protein. Snack B has twice the protein per 100g, so you eat less of it to get the same protein. Snack A gives more protein per rupee. Which one is right depends on whether you care more about budget or about how much you need to eat. Remember to add shipping to the price if you buy online.

The categories, one by one

Roasted chana

Roasted chana is one of the cheapest protein snacks in India, sold loose and in packs almost everywhere. It has about 22g of protein per 100g. It is dry and plain, so many people add masala, lemon or onion. If budget is your main goal, it is hard to beat on protein per rupee.

Makhana

Makhana is popular because it is light, crunchy and easy to flavour. Published analyses put popped makhana at roughly 8g to 12g of protein per 100g. That is useful, but it is lower than chana, peanuts or soya. Choose makhana for a light crunch, not as your main protein snack. Check how much oil or ghee is used in roasted and flavoured packs.

Protein bars

Protein bars are convenient, but they differ a lot. Some get most of their protein from whey or soy isolate, others from nuts and dates. Look at three things: grams of protein per bar, grams of sugar per bar, and the first three ingredients. A bar that lists sugar, syrup or dates first is closer to a sweet snack.

Nuts and peanuts

Peanuts (about 24g per 100g) and almonds (about 21g per 100g) have good protein, but they are also high in fat, so a small handful goes a long way. Peanuts usually cost much less than almonds, so they give more protein per rupee. Watch for fried and heavily salted versions.

Soya and edamame snacks

Soybeans are among the highest protein plant foods. Dry roasted soybeans have about 43g of protein per 100g. Edamame is young soybean. Fresh cooked edamame has about 12g per 100g because it holds a lot of water. Once edamame is roasted and dried, the protein per 100g rises sharply. Soya snacks can be roasted or fried, so check how a pack is made.

A quick checklist before you buy

  1. Read protein in the per 100g column, not per serving.
  2. Work out protein per ₹100, including shipping.
  3. Check whether it is roasted or fried, and which oil is used.
  4. Check sugar (for bars) and salt (for namkeen style snacks).
  5. Pick a mix: one budget option, like chana, and one you enjoy enough to keep in your bag.

Frequently asked questions

Which snack has the most protein per 100g in India under ₹499?

Among common snack types, roasted soya and roasted edamame are usually the highest. Plain dry roasted soybeans have about 43g of protein per 100g. Roasted chana and peanuts follow at about 22g to 24g per 100g. Packaged snacks vary, so always check the label.

Is makhana a good source of protein?

Makhana has some protein, roughly 8g to 12g per 100g in published analyses. That is less than roasted chana, peanuts or soya snacks. It is a good light snack, but not a high-protein one.

How do I compare protein value between two snacks?

Multiply protein per 100g by the pack weight and divide by 100 to get total protein. Divide that by the price and multiply by 100 to get protein per ₹100. Add shipping if you buy online.
