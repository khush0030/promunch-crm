Short answer: frying cooks food in hot oil, and the food soaks up some of that oil, so fried snacks usually carry much more fat than the raw ingredient. Roasting cooks food with dry heat, sometimes with a little oil. Protein per 100g changes very little either way. Both methods use high heat, and "roasted" on a pack does not always mean "no oil". Read the label to know what you are getting.

What each method actually does

Frying Roasting
How it works Food is cooked in hot oil, usually fully dipped (deep frying) Food is cooked with dry hot air or a hot pan or drum
Oil used Always. Some oil stays in the food. None (dry roasting) or a small amount brushed or tossed on
Water Driven out fast, replaced partly by oil Driven out slowly
Texture Light, crisp, classic chip crunch Harder, nuttier crunch
Typical Indian examples Namkeen, bhujia, chips, fried peanuts Bhuna chana, roasted makhana, dry roasted peanuts

What changes: fat

Fat is the biggest difference. A simple example from USDA data: raw potato has less than 0.1g of fat per 100g, but plain salted potato chips have about 34g of fat per 100g. Almost all of that fat comes from the frying oil.

Roasting with oil also adds some fat, but usually less. USDA data for almonds shows this clearly:

Almonds, per 100g Fat Protein
Natural (not roasted) 49.9g 21.2g
Dry roasted 52.5g 21.0g
Oil roasted 55.2g 21.2g

Two things stand out. Oil roasting added a few grams of fat. And dry roasting raised fat per 100g a little too, because water left the nut and everything else became more concentrated.

What barely changes: protein

In the almond table above, protein stays at about 21g per 100g whether the nuts are raw, dry roasted or oil roasted. That is typical. Heat changes the texture of protein, but it does not remove it. If a roasted and a fried version of the same snack have very different protein numbers, the difference usually comes from the recipe (coatings, flour, seasoning), not the cooking method. Compare labels, not cooking methods, for protein.

What both methods share: high heat

Frying, roasting and baking all cook food at high temperatures. When starchy foods are cooked above about 120°C with little moisture, a natural browning reaction can form a compound called acrylamide. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) both note that it forms in frying, roasting and baking. So "roasted" is not a free pass. For home cooking, EFSA advises cooking starchy foods to a golden yellow colour rather than brown.

What the word "roasted" does not tell you

  • Whether oil was used. Many roasted snacks are roasted with some oil. Check the ingredients list for oil and its type.
  • How much salt there is. Roasted and fried snacks can both be salty. Check sodium per 100g.
  • What else is in it. Coatings, flours and seasoning change the nutrition more than people expect.

How to read a snack label in 30 seconds

  1. Read the name and the ingredients. Look for the words "fried", "roasted" or "baked", and for oil in the ingredients.
  2. Check fat per 100g. This is where frying usually shows up.
  3. Check protein per 100g if protein is why you are buying it.
  4. Check sodium per 100g.
  5. Use the per 100g column to compare two packs, not the per serving column.

So which should you pick?

Pick based on what you want from the snack. If you want less added oil, a roasted snack is usually the better match, as long as the label agrees. If you want the light, airy crunch of a chip, a fried snack will give you that, and it is fine to enjoy it knowing what it is. Either way, compare labels per 100g, and keep an eye on portion size.

Frequently asked questions

Does roasting reduce protein?

Not in any meaningful way. USDA data for almonds shows about 21g of protein per 100g whether they are raw, dry roasted or oil roasted. Roasting removes water, so protein per 100g can even rise slightly.

Do roasted snacks have no oil?

Not always. Dry roasting uses no oil, but many roasted snacks are roasted with a little oil. Check the ingredients list.

Are all PROMUNCH snacks roasted?

No. PROMUNCH Roasted Edamame is roasted in olive oil, and Soya Crunchies are roasted, not fried. Soya Sticks and Soya Chips are fried.

Why are fried chips so much higher in fat than potatoes?

Raw potato has less than 0.1g of fat per 100g. Frying replaces water in the potato slices with oil, so plain salted potato chips end up with about 34g of fat per 100g.
